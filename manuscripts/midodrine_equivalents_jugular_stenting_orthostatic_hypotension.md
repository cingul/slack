# Midodrine Equivalents as a Medication-Burden Endpoint After Supracardiac Venous Angioplasty and Stenting for Orthostatic Hypotension: The STANDUP Framework

## Abstract

**Background:** Orthostatic hypotension (OH) is usually evaluated with orthostatic blood pressure change and symptom burden. These measures may miss an important clinical response: reduced dependence on vasoactive medications. Standard OH therapies, including midodrine, droxidopa, fludrocortisone, and pyridostigmine, are often limited by incomplete efficacy, cost, supine hypertension, urinary adverse effects, fluid retention, electrolyte abnormalities, and other treatment-limiting toxicities. The STANDUP program evaluates whether supracardiac venous outflow obstruction is a modifiable contributor to refractory OH and orthostatic intolerance.

**Objective:** To define Midodrine Equivalents (MEQ) as a standardized medication-burden endpoint and apply it to patients undergoing supracardiac venous angioplasty and/or stenting for refractory OH.

**Methods:** Source STANDUP materials define MEQ by assigning 100 MEQ to the usual maximum daily dose of each OH-directed medication: midodrine 30 mg/day, droxidopa 1800 mg/day, fludrocortisone 0.3 mg/day, and pyridostigmine 180 mg/day. A patient's total MEQ is the sum of medication-specific MEQ values. In the STANDUP clinical cohort, adults with refractory OH underwent endovascular evaluation and treatment of supracardiac venous outflow obstruction. Outcomes included change in orthostatic systolic blood pressure (SBP) drop, change in MEQ among patients receiving vasoactive medication at baseline, Clinical Global Impression-Improvement (CGI-I), angiographic time-density curve (TDC) parameters, and safety events.

**Results:** In the source STANDUP draft, 52 patients underwent supracardiac venous angioplasty and/or stenting. Paired orthostatic blood pressure data were available for 48 patients. Mean orthostatic SBP drop decreased from 36.2 +/- 21.9 mmHg to 26.3 +/- 19.1 mmHg, a mean improvement of 9.9 mmHg (95% CI, 4.0 to 15.8; p=0.0007). Among 24 patients receiving vasoactive therapy at baseline, mean MEQ decreased from 124.2 to 39.7, corresponding to a 68.0% reduction. Eleven of 24 patients (45.8%) achieved complete medication independence, and 18 of 24 (75.0%) achieved at least a 50% MEQ reduction. Clinically meaningful global improvement was reported in a majority of patients. One intervention-related major adverse event occurred: bilateral internal jugular vein thrombosis requiring recanalization and supplemental treatment, with substantial resolution of associated hypoglossal nerve palsy.

**Conclusions:** MEQ provides a reproducible way to quantify medication de-escalation after intervention for refractory OH. In the STANDUP source cohort, supracardiac venous angioplasty and/or stenting was associated with improvement in orthostatic SBP regulation and substantial reduction in medication burden. Because the data are single-arm and observational, controlled studies are needed to confirm causality, validate MEQ against patient-centered outcomes, and identify which venous outflow phenotypes are most likely to respond.

## Key messages

### What is already known

- OH is defined by a sustained fall in blood pressure after standing or head-up tilt and is associated with falls, functional impairment, cardiovascular morbidity, and mortality.
- Current OH medications improve symptoms in some patients but frequently create treatment burden through adverse effects, polypharmacy, cost, and supine hypertension.
- Medication reduction is clinically meaningful but is usually reported narratively rather than with a standardized quantitative endpoint.

### What this manuscript adds

- MEQ normalizes OH-directed medication use to a common scale in which each medication's usual maximum daily dose equals 100 MEQ.
- The STANDUP framework combines three response domains: orthostatic SBP drop, symptoms/global clinical status, and MEQ reduction.
- In the source STANDUP cohort, medication burden decreased by 68.0% among baseline medication users, with 45.8% achieving medication independence.

### How this might affect research and practice

- MEQ can be incorporated into prospective OH studies to distinguish true medication-sparing improvement from apparent stability maintained by escalating pharmacotherapy.
- Future trials of supracardiac venous intervention should prespecify MEQ, symptom instruments such as OHQ or CGI-I, orthostatic hemodynamics, venous imaging criteria, and safety endpoints.

## Introduction

Orthostatic hypotension is defined by consensus as a sustained reduction in systolic blood pressure of at least 20 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure of at least 10 mmHg within 3 minutes of standing or head-up tilt [@freeman2011consensus; @gibbons2017consensus]. OH causes dizziness, presyncope, syncope, falls, weakness, fatigue, cognitive difficulty, visual symptoms, and loss of independence. It is common in older adults and in patients with autonomic disorders, and it has been associated with cardiovascular morbidity and mortality [@ricci2015meta; @rutan1992chs].

Management usually begins with medication review, hydration, salt intake when appropriate, compression strategies, counter-maneuvers, and avoidance of provoking factors. Pharmacologic therapy is often required when symptoms persist. Midodrine and droxidopa have the strongest evidence among approved pressor agents for neurogenic OH, while fludrocortisone, pyridostigmine, and other agents are used in selected patients with weaker or more heterogeneous evidence [@fanciulli2017evidence; @gibbons2017consensus; @kulkarni2024treating]. These drugs can be helpful, but they do not eliminate the underlying disorder and can be limited by supine hypertension, urinary retention, edema, hypokalemia, metabolic alkalosis, heart failure exacerbation, headache, nausea, palpitations, fatigue, and financial burden.

Standard OH endpoints incompletely capture this treatment burden. A patient may have a smaller orthostatic SBP drop because medication has been intensified. Conversely, a patient may have stable blood pressure and improved symptoms while tapering from multiple agents to no therapy. The latter outcome is meaningful to patients, clinicians, and payers, but without a standardized medication-burden score it is difficult to analyze or compare across patients.

The STANDUP program is based on the hypothesis that supracardiac venous outflow obstruction, particularly involving the internal jugular and brachiocephalic veins, may contribute to refractory orthostatic syndromes in selected patients. Proposed mechanisms include impaired venous return and preload, abnormal cerebral venous pressure dynamics, impaired clearance of neuroactive metabolites from central autonomic networks, vestibulo-sympathetic reflex dysfunction, abnormal venous tone, and impaired glymphatic or perivenous clearance. These mechanisms remain incompletely proven, but they provide a biologically plausible framework for prospective testing.

This manuscript presents MEQ as a standardized medication-burden endpoint and integrates it into a publication-ready STANDUP outcomes framework. The goal is not to claim pharmacologic equivalence across drugs, but to make medication reduction measurable, auditable, and analyzable.

## Methods

### Study design and source materials

This draft is based on source materials added to the repository: the STANDUP 1 manuscript draft, the STANDUP 2 protocol, the STANDUP 2 case report form, the MEQ metric document, and a medication de-escalation methods document. These materials describe a prospective single-arm clinical investigation of supracardiac venous angioplasty and/or stenting for refractory OH, as well as a prospective registry framework for future enrollment.

The manuscript should be finalized against the locked analytic dataset, final IRB language, final author list, and final journal requirements before submission.

### Study population

The STANDUP clinical cohort included adults with severe, function-limiting OH refractory to or not amenable to standard medical therapy. In the source STANDUP draft, 52 consecutive patients underwent supracardiac venous angioplasty and/or stenting. OH was defined using established criteria as a systolic blood pressure fall of at least 20 mmHg on transition from supine to standing posture. Orthostatic intolerance was considered a broader upright intolerance syndrome characterized by dizziness, presyncope, fatigue, visual disturbance, or syncope relieved by recumbency.

For STANDUP 2, the protocol specifies adult patients with OH or orthostatic intolerance by OHQ score greater than 3, plus severe stenosis in the supracardiac venous outflow tract draining the head and neck. Candidate venous territories include the transverse and sigmoid sinuses, internal jugular veins, and brachiocephalic veins.

### Venous outflow eligibility criteria

The STANDUP 2 protocol classifies severe supracardiac venous stenosis using one or more of the following categories:

1. **Radiographic stenosis:** greater than 50% reduction in lumen diameter compared with adjacent normal segment or expected normal anatomy, plus supportive features such as contrast stagnation greater than 10 seconds, reflux into collaterals, varicosities near valves, filling defects, thrombus, septae, or webs.
2. **IVUS-based stenosis:** greater than 75% reduction in cross-sectional area compared with adjacent normal segment, plus supportive features such as smoke, increased speckling density, abnormal valve mobility or thickening, thrombus, or recanalized thrombus.
3. **Pressure gradient:** at least 3 mmHg across the lesion.
4. **Pressure waveform abnormality:** damped or monophasic waveform.

These criteria should be reported explicitly in future publications because patient selection is central to interpreting outcomes.

### Endovascular evaluation and intervention

Source materials describe diagnostic venography, venous pressure assessment, and intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) focused on the internal jugular veins and brachiocephalic veins. In the STANDUP 1 draft, procedures were commonly performed with spontaneous respiration under laryngeal mask airway anesthesia. After transfemoral venous access, guide catheter placement, pressure waveform acquisition, microwire navigation, and high-definition IVUS were performed from the superior sagittal sinus through the superior vena cava. Jugular venography was obtained above C1 and at mid-jugular levels. Treatment included angioplasty and/or stenting, most commonly with Medtronic Abre stents and post-deployment angioplasty. Of 52 treated patients in the source draft, 3 underwent angioplasty alone, 10 unilateral stenting, and 39 bilateral stenting.

### Orthostatic blood pressure assessment

Orthostatic blood pressure was assessed in supine, seated, and standing positions with an automated upper-arm cuff at heart level. Orthostatic SBP drop was calculated as supine SBP minus standing SBP. Where multiple pre- or post-intervention values were available, values were averaged in the source draft to generate representative pre- and post-treatment measures. Patients unable to tolerate standing because of presyncope or syncope were assigned a prespecified standing blood pressure value of 50/20 mmHg in the source analysis to represent profound OH while avoiding physiologically implausible values.

For future protocolized work, the measurement conditions should be standardized whenever possible: time of day, hydration status, meal timing, compression garment use, last medication dose, standing duration, and reason for early termination.

### Medication burden and MEQ calculation

MEQ quantifies the total daily burden of OH-directed vasoactive therapy. The source MEQ document defines 100 MEQ as the usual maximum daily dose for each of four common OH medications:

| Medication | Usual maximum daily dose | Conversion factor | Formula |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Midodrine | 30 mg/day | 3.3333 MEQ/mg | daily mg x 3.3333 |
| Fludrocortisone | 0.3 mg/day | 333.3333 MEQ/mg | daily mg x 333.3333 |
| Droxidopa | 1800 mg/day | 0.055556 MEQ/mg | daily mg x 0.055556 |
| Pyridostigmine | 180 mg/day | 0.55556 MEQ/mg | daily mg x 0.55556 |

Total MEQ is calculated by summing the medication-specific MEQ values. A patient on maximum doses of all four medications would have 400 MEQ, although such regimens are uncommon. This framework assumes dose proportionality within usual ranges and equal weighting of maximum daily dose across medications. Those assumptions are pragmatic and should be tested in sensitivity analyses.

### Medication de-escalation framework

The source de-escalation document and STANDUP 2 protocol describe a pragmatic clinician-directed sequence after intervention:

1. droxidopa;
2. pyridostigmine;
3. fludrocortisone;
4. midodrine.

This sequence was chosen based on cost, side-effect profiles, and clinical reversibility. Droxidopa was prioritized because of high cost and variable response. Midodrine was retained longest because of rapid onset, short duration, and usefulness as a rescue or titratable agent. At each step, if the patient was taking the next medication, it was stopped or tapered according to clinical judgment; if not, the protocol advanced to the next drug. Orthostatic vital signs and symptom checks were used to determine tolerability. If intolerance occurred, the protocol was halted and a slower individualized taper was implemented.

This sequence should be presented as a pragmatic management framework, not as a validated de-escalation algorithm.

### Clinical, angiographic, and safety outcomes

The source STANDUP draft reports the following outcomes:

- change in orthostatic SBP drop;
- change in total MEQ among patients receiving vasoactive therapy at baseline;
- complete medication independence, defined as MEQ = 0;
- at least 50% reduction in total MEQ;
- Clinical Global Impression-Improvement (CGI-I);
- angiographic time-density curve (TDC) parameters, including time-to-peak, mean transit time, and exponential decay constant;
- major adverse events, device performance, thrombotic events, hemorrhagic events, neurologic complications, device migration, deployment failure, and procedure-related mortality.

The STANDUP 2 protocol also includes OHQ, quality-of-life assessments, facial images, infrared facial temperature measurements, right heart catheterization measures, and longitudinal safety follow-up.

### Statistical analysis

Continuous outcomes should be summarized as mean +/- standard deviation or median with interquartile range as appropriate. Paired pre/post analyses should use paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests depending on distribution. Categorical outcomes should be summarized as counts and percentages. MEQ analyses should be limited to patients taking vasoactive medication at baseline, with a separate analysis including all participants if desired. Associations among SBP drop, MEQ, TDC parameters, and CGI-I should be considered exploratory unless prespecified and adequately powered.

## Results

### Study cohort

The source STANDUP draft included 52 treated patients, 33 male and 19 female, with age ranging from 49 to 93 years (mean 75.3 years; SD 9.7; median 76). Paired orthostatic blood pressure data were available for 48 patients. Twenty-four patients were receiving vasoactive therapy at baseline and were included in medication-burden analyses. CGI-I scores were available for 49 patients.

### Orthostatic blood pressure outcomes

Orthostatic SBP regulation improved after intervention. Mean orthostatic SBP drop decreased from 36.2 +/- 21.9 mmHg before treatment to 26.3 +/- 19.1 mmHg after treatment. This corresponded to a mean reduction of 9.9 mmHg (95% CI, 4.0 to 15.8; p=0.0007). Directional improvement in orthostatic SBP regulation was reported in 35 of 48 patients (72.9%).

ROC analysis in the source draft suggested that a 20.4% improvement in orthostatic SBP drop was the optimal threshold for predicting symptomatic improvement, with sensitivity 64.1% and specificity 87.5% (Fisher exact p=0.047). This threshold should be treated as hypothesis-generating until externally validated.

### Medication burden outcomes

Among baseline medication users (n=24), mean MEQ decreased from 124.2 to 39.7, representing a 68.0% reduction in medication burden. The mean absolute MEQ reduction was 80.9 (95% CI, 43.6 to 118.2; p<0.0001). Eleven of 24 patients (45.8%) discontinued OH-directed vasoactive medications entirely. Eighteen of 24 patients (75.0%) achieved at least a 50% reduction in total MEQ.

These findings support medication burden as a clinically meaningful endpoint distinct from orthostatic cuff blood pressure alone.

### Angiographic outcomes

The source draft reports significant pre/post differences in several TDC parameters. Time-to-peak differed significantly on the right internal jugular vein but not on the left or bi-jugular average. The exponential decay constant differed significantly on the right, left, and bi-jugular average. Mean transit time differed significantly on the left and bi-jugular average but not on the right. Because mean transit time and exponential decay constant were strongly correlated, subsequent interpretation focused on the exponential decay constant as a functional proxy for venous washout.

Univariate correlations between SBP drop, MEQ, and exponential decay constant were not statistically significant in the source draft. Multivariable modeling suggested a positive signal when angiographic and SBP measures were considered together, but this requires confirmation in larger samples.

### Clinical outcomes

The source draft reports clinically meaningful global improvement in a majority of patients. It also describes discordance between peripheral blood pressure improvement and symptom improvement: some patients improved symptomatically without large measured SBP changes, while others had better SBP drops without parallel symptom benefit. This supports a multidomain endpoint strategy rather than reliance on any single measure.

### Safety

No procedure-related mortality was reported in the 52-patient source cohort. One intervention-related major adverse event (1.9%) occurred: bilateral internal jugular vein thrombosis, treated with recanalization, supplemental stenting, and coiling of the left condylar emissary vein. An associated ipsilateral hypoglossal nerve palsy substantially resolved during follow-up. One additional serious non-device-related peri-procedural event occurred: COPD exacerbation likely related to intubation, requiring inpatient admission and resolving by the next day. No vessel perforation, deployment failure, acute stent fracture, intra-procedural stent migration, significant access-site complication, or systemic hemorrhagic event was reported in the source draft.

## Discussion

This manuscript advances two related ideas. First, medication burden should be treated as a core endpoint in OH intervention studies. Second, the STANDUP data suggest that selected patients with refractory OH and supracardiac venous outflow obstruction may experience improvement across hemodynamic, pharmacologic, angiographic, and clinical domains after angioplasty and/or stenting.

The medication findings are especially important. Among patients taking vasoactive therapy at baseline, MEQ fell by 68.0%, nearly half achieved medication independence, and three quarters achieved at least a 50% reduction. These outcomes matter because OH medications can impose major daily burden even when they help symptoms. Reducing or stopping droxidopa can reduce financial burden. Reducing fludrocortisone can reduce risks related to fluid retention, hypokalemia, and heart failure. Reducing midodrine can reduce supine hypertension, urinary retention, paresthesia, and dosing complexity.

MEQ makes these changes visible. Narrative descriptions such as "medications were reduced" do not distinguish a small dose change from complete discontinuation of multiple agents. MEQ allows each patient's medication course to be plotted over time, correlated with symptoms and blood pressure, and analyzed in aggregate.

### Why MEQ should accompany, not replace, SBP and symptoms

MEQ is not a standalone success measure. A reduction in medication burden is favorable only if the patient remains hemodynamically and clinically stable. Medication reduction accompanied by recurrent syncope, worsening OHQ, falls, or severe orthostatic SBP drop is not a therapeutic success. Conversely, stable MEQ with large improvements in SBP drop and symptoms may represent a meaningful hemodynamic response even without de-escalation.

A robust response framework should therefore include:

1. objective orthostatic hemodynamics;
2. patient-centered symptoms and function, preferably OHQ plus global clinical assessment;
3. MEQ medication burden;
4. safety endpoints including supine hypertension, syncope, falls, thrombotic events, neurologic complications, and stent patency.

### Mechanistic interpretation

The proposed mechanism is multidimensional. Relief of venous outflow obstruction may improve venous return and preload during upright posture. It may also reduce upstream venous congestion, improve cerebral venous drainage, and improve metabolic conditions in brainstem and vestibular autonomic networks. These changes could enhance baroreflex and vestibulo-sympathetic responses, improve cerebral perfusion reserve, and reduce the need for downstream pharmacologic compensation.

These mechanisms are plausible but not proven. The single-arm design cannot exclude placebo effects, regression to the mean, medication-management effects, patient selection, measurement variability, or natural fluctuation in OH. Nonetheless, the combination of SBP improvement, medication reduction, and angiographic washout changes provides a coherent positive signal worthy of controlled evaluation.

### Positioning within the literature

Prior OH literature emphasizes the diagnostic definition of OH, the importance of standardized orthostatic vitals, non-pharmacologic measures, and careful medication selection [@freeman2011consensus; @gibbons2017consensus; @fanciulli2017evidence]. The OHQ provides a validated symptom and daily activity instrument [@kaufmann2012ohq]. Venous outflow literature describes internal jugular stenosis, dynamic compression, venous stenting, and improvement in selected neurologic syndromes, although indications and outcomes remain heterogeneous [@bai2023stenting; @higgins2015brain; @scerrati2021jugular]. The STANDUP framework adds a medication-burden endpoint that can be used across this emerging research area.

### Limitations

This draft has several limitations. The source STANDUP cohort is single-arm and observational, limiting causal inference. The cohort came from a specialized referral environment and may not generalize to unselected OH populations. Follow-up timing, blood pressure measurement windows, medication tapering, and symptom assessments varied across patients. CGI-I was not the same as a blinded validated OH-specific symptom scale, and OHQ should be emphasized in future prospective work. MEQ assumes proportional dose-response and equal weighting of maximum daily doses across medications despite different mechanisms, pharmacokinetics, adverse-effect profiles, and evidence levels. TDC metrics were retrospectively post-processed and need prospective standardization. Finally, long-term stent patency and durability of medication independence require continued follow-up.

## Proposed responder framework for future STANDUP analyses

| Response domain | Proposed measure | Candidate response definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hemodynamics | Orthostatic SBP drop | At least 20% improvement or no longer meeting OH criteria |
| Symptoms/function | OHQ composite and/or CGI-I | OHQ improvement of at least 1 point or CGI-I <= 2/3 |
| Medication burden | MEQ | At least 50% reduction or MEQ = 0 |
| Safety | Falls, syncope, supine hypertension, adverse events | No clinically important worsening |
| Venous outflow | TDC exponential decay constant, MTT, TTP, venography/IVUS | Improved washout or anatomic/hemodynamic correction |

A complete multidomain response could be defined as improvement in SBP drop, improvement in OHQ/CGI-I, at least 50% MEQ reduction, and no safety deterioration. A medication-sparing response could be defined as stable or improved SBP drop and symptoms with at least 50% MEQ reduction.

## Conclusion

MEQ is a practical and transparent medication-burden endpoint for OH research. In the STANDUP source cohort, supracardiac venous angioplasty and/or stenting was associated with improved orthostatic SBP regulation and substantial reduction in vasoactive medication burden, including complete medication independence in nearly half of baseline medication users. These findings support further prospective, controlled evaluation of supracardiac venous outflow obstruction as a modifiable contributor to refractory orthostatic syndromes and support MEQ as a core endpoint in that work.
